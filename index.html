    FENSKE EQUATION · TOTAL REFLUX

    What is the fewest number of stages?

    Return all the condensed liquid to the column. At steady total reflux the column has no feed or product withdrawal. The vapor from each contact passes its enrichment to the next one.

    — minimum theoretical stages

    Nmin = ln[(xD,LK / xD,HK) / (xB,LK / xB,HK)] / ln(αLK / αHK)

    xD, xB are liquid mole fractions in the distillate and bottoms. LK and HK identify the light and heavy keys.

    Derive the equation

    At equilibrium, yi = Kixi. Divide the light key by the heavy key and the vapor ratio is αLK/αHK times the liquid ratio. Here x means liquid mole fraction and y means vapor mole fraction.

    With zero product withdrawal, the total-reflux balance matches the compositions of vapor and liquid passing each other between stages. That passes one stage’s enrichment to the next. After N contacts, the ratio has been multiplied by (αLK/αHK)N. Take logs to recover N. Withdrawing enriched product reduces the separation each contact can pass along. That is why this total-reflux calculation gives a lower bound for the stage count.

    UNDERWOOD EQUATIONS · MINIMUM REFLUX

    What is the least reflux?

    Reduce the liquid sent back. Eventually, there is a pinch: the composition barely changes from one tray to the next. At the limiting reflux, an infinite number of stages would be needed to cross that region.

    — minimum reflux ratio

    Σ αizi / (αi − θ) = 1 − q
    Rmin + 1 = Σ αixD,i / (αi − θ)

    zi is component i’s feed fraction. q is the liquid fraction in this version of the model. θ is the value that makes the feed equation balance.

    Where do the equations come from?

    A component balance above the feed says V yi = L xi + D xD,i. At a pinch the compositions repeat, so the liquid and vapor there also satisfy yi = Kixi. Substituting makes each component contribute a term with a denominator proportional to αi − θ.

    The pinch balances above and below the feed are represented by the common Underwood root θ in this shortcut. Combining their sums replaces D xD,i + B xB,i with F zi by the overall component balance. Dividing by F gives the feed equation. The right-hand side is 1 − q because the feed changes the vapor flow by (1 − q)F. Within the key bracket, the feed sum strictly increases and runs from negative to positive infinity. That is why bisection has one crossing to find there. For our assumed split, solve for θ between the heavy- and light-key volatilities.

    The condenser balance V = L + D gives V/D = R + 1. The second sum gives this overhead vapor divided by distillate at minimum reflux.

    The equation crosses zero between the key volatilities.

    Why use normalized axes?

    X and Y measure how far the operating choice is from each limit on a common 0-to-1 scale.

    X = (R − Rmin) / (R + 1)
    Y = (N − Nmin) / (N + 1)

    At R → Rmin, X → 0 and N → ∞, so Y → 1. At total reflux, R → ∞ and X → 1, while N → Nmin and Y → 0. Those endpoints follow from the definitions. The curve between them is fit to design data. One exponential fit is shown below.

    Y = 1 − exp[((1 + 54.4X) / (11 + 117.2X)) · ((X − 1) / √X)]

    Model notes

    This shortcut model uses constant relative volatilities with the heavy key as the reference (αHK = 1), constant molar overflow, a total condenser, and an equilibrium reboiler. Gilliland’s stage count includes the equilibrium reboiler. For tray allocation, the lab rounds up and allows at least one feed tray plus the reboiler. The specified fraction of inter-tray positions above the feed sets its location by rounding to the nearest tray. Starting catalog values use a rough boiling-point estimate. Use relative volatilities appropriate to the mixture, temperature, and pressure for engineering calculations.

    Equilibrium and feed state

    With constant relative volatilities, yi = αixi / Σ αjxj. For an ideal mixture at a given temperature, Raoult’s law relates α to the ratio of saturation pressures, which can be estimated from Antoine’s equation. For a partially vaporized feed, q = 1 − β, where β is the vapor fraction from a flash calculation.

    Fenske at total reflux

    The ratio of light to heavy key in the distillate divided by that ratio in the bottoms determines Nmin.

    Underwood at minimum reflux

    Find θ between 1 and α from Σ αizi / (αi − θ) = 1 − q. Then evaluate Rmin + 1 = Σ αixD,i / (αi − θ). Here q is the liquid fraction of the feed in the selected range, from saturated vapor (0) to saturated liquid (1). The reflux multiplier requires a positive Rmin.

    Gilliland at finite reflux

    Set X = (R − Rmin) / (R + 1) and Y = (N − Nmin) / (N + 1). The exponential fit to the Gilliland correlation gives Y from X.

    Y = 1 − exp[((1 + 54.4X) / (11 + 117.2X)) · ((X − 1) / √X)]

    Recover N = (Nmin + Y) / (1 − Y). The design curve approaches the minimum stage count as reflux increases and rises sharply as reflux approaches Rmin. The normalized view shows both endpoints directly.
