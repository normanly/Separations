Degrees of freedom

Subtract the number of independent equations from the number of unknowns. The result is the number of independent values you must supply. In the course column model, the balances and equipment assumptions leave four design choices. In this example they are:

Light-key recovery
Send 95% of the n-hexane in the feed to the distillate.
Heavy-key recovery
Send 96% of the n-heptane in the feed to the bottoms.
Operating reflux
Return 1.50 times the minimum reflux ratio.
Feed tray position
Place the feed near the middle of the estimated trays.

Feed composition and quality define the problem. In a four-component feed, three mole fractions are independent because the fourth follows from the requirement that they sum to one. Changing the feed creates a new case with the same four design choices.

Worked degrees-of-freedom calculation

Degrees of freedom equal unknowns minus independent equations. This count uses the course column with a rectifying section, a stripping section, one feed tray, a condenser, a reflux drum that splits liquid, and an equilibrium reboiler.

Assemble the column

Let C be the number of components (currently 4), and let T be the number of rectifying and stripping trays combined, excluding the feed tray and reboiler. The standalone counts use the course convention.

Start with n equilibrium trays. Each neighboring pair shares a vapor and a liquid stream. Subtract those repeated variables and add one choice for the section tray count.

n(2C + 6) − 2(n − 1)(C + 2) + 1 = 2n + 2C + 5

Add both sections, the feed tray, condenser, reboiler, and reflux splitter.

[2T + 4C + 10] + [3C + 8] + 2[C + 4] + [C + 5] = 2T + 10C + 31

Count the nine streams shared between those units once. Three are the overhead vapor, condensate, and reflux. The other six are the paired liquid and vapor links on either side of the feed tray and at the reboiler. Each shared stream has C + 2 independent variables: total flow, C − 1 mole fractions, temperature, and pressure.

2T + 10C + 31 − 9(C + 2) = 2T + C + 13

Why the inlet is editable

The inlet uses C + 2 specifications: one flow basis, C − 1 independent mole fractions, and two thermal-state properties (temperature and pressure in the course count). Each run fixes these values. Editing them defines a new feed case with the same number of independent design choices. The lab fixes the flow basis at 100 mol and represents inlet state through feed quality q. A real feed temperature and pressure would be used in a flash calculation to obtain that quality.

  • Composition. With four components, three feed mole fractions are independent. The fourth equals 100% minus their sum. The lab fills in the final fraction to keep the total at 100%. At fixed recoveries, a different feed composition changes the product flows and compositions and can change minimum reflux.
  • Feed quality. Changing q changes the right-hand side 1 − q of Underwood’s feed equation. It can therefore change the minimum reflux and the operating stage estimate. With key recoveries and relative volatilities held fixed, Fenske’s minimum stages stay the same.
Back to the inlet and design inputs

Split the feed by component

Start with 100 mol, with 25 mol of each hydrocarbon. A key recovery is the fraction of that component in the feed that reaches its specified product. For example, 25 × 0.95 = 23.75 mol of n-hexane goes to the distillate. The remaining 1.25 mol goes to the bottoms.

To get a product mole fraction, divide the component moles by the total moles in that product. The distillate is 23.75 ÷ 49.75 = 47.74 mol% n-hexane. Its n-hexane recovery is 23.75 ÷ 25 = 95%.

Fenske gives the fewest stages

At total reflux, the condenser returns all liquid to the column. No product leaves. In one ideal contact, the vapor’s light-to-heavy key ratio is the liquid’s ratio multiplied by the relative volatility of the two keys.

(yLK/yHK) = (αLK/αHK) × (xLK/xHK)

With total reflux, the balance passes that enrichment from stage to stage. If each stage multiplies the ratio by 2, then N stages multiply it by 2N. Here, the distillate key ratio is 23.75 ÷ 1.00 and the bottoms key ratio is 1.25 ÷ 24.00. The required enrichment is 456-fold.

2Nₘᵢₙ = 456 → Nmin = ln(456) / ln(2) = 8.83 stages

Fenske supplies the lower stage limit for the specified products and the assumed constant relative volatilities. Watch the stages enrich the light key

Underwood gives the least reflux

Near minimum reflux, the vapor and liquid compositions change very little from one stage to the next. At the pinch, those steps tend to zero. The mathematical limit requires infinitely many stages to achieve the separation.

The feed quality q is the liquid fraction of the feed in this lab. The example is half liquid, so q = 0.50. The remaining half enters as vapor, which is why the feed equation uses 1 − q.

Σ [αi zi / (αi − θ)] = 1 − q

Here zi is the feed mole fraction of component i. Solve for θ in the interval between the heavy- and light-key volatilities, 1 < θ < 2. The left side increases throughout that interval, so you can repeatedly halve the interval to find its one crossing. The example gives θ ≈ 1.39309.

The same θ enters the equation for the overhead stream. The condenser balance is V = L + D, where V is overhead vapor, L is returned liquid and D is distillate. Divide by D to get V/D = R + 1. Use the distillate mole fractions xD,i in the sum:

Rmin + 1 = Σ [αi xD,i / (αi − θ)] ≈ 2.65698 → Rmin ≈ 1.65698

Compare the pinch with a higher-reflux case

Gilliland estimates stages at working reflux

Choose a finite reflux above the minimum. In this example, R = 1.50 × Rmin ≈ 2.48547. The correlation uses scaled distances from the two limits:

X = (R − Rmin) / (R + 1)
Y = (N − Nmin) / (N + 1)

As reflux approaches the minimum, X approaches 0 and the required stages rise toward infinity, so Y approaches 1. As reflux tends to infinity, X approaches 1 and N approaches Nmin, so Y approaches 0. The curve between these limits is an empirical fit.

X ≈ 0.23770 → Y ≈ 0.42910 → N = (Nmin + Y) / (1 − Y) ≈ 16.22

Round up to 17 equilibrium stages. This includes one reboiler, so the tray column has 16 ideal trays. Trace the trade-off on the interactive chart

Model assumptions

The shortcut uses constant relative volatility, constant molar overflow, a total condenser, and an equilibrium reboiler. Components more volatile than the light key go entirely to the distillate. Components less volatile than the heavy key go entirely to the bottoms. The sample volatilities are illustrative, and real equipment design requires additional thermodynamic and hydraulic checks.
